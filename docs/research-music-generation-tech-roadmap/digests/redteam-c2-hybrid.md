# Red-team C2: two-layer symbolic + contour hybrid vs end-to-end audio

Conclusion under attack: "For a controllable music generator meant to cover culturally diverse traditions, the best architecture is a two-layer hybrid. A discrete symbolic note/score layer carries control, and a continuous pitch-contour/performance layer rendered to audio carries expressive and non-12-TET detail. This beats generating audio end-to-end."

Budget used: 15 tool calls (1 ToolSearch, 7 WebSearch, 7 WebFetch). Run date: 2026-11-19. Most fetches read only abstract pages, so full-paper details are unverified.

## Verdict

**Weakened, not overturned.** I found no head-to-head study showing end-to-end audio beats a symbolic-plus-contour hybrid on control or quality, for any tradition. The strongest 2026 evidence partly supports the conclusion. In the YuE2 ablation, experts preferred the output when the model first wrote an explicit, readable score (49.3% vs 34.6%). But three findings undercut parts of the claim.

1. **Separate stages lost to a joint model.** In YuE2, experts preferred one jointly trained model that does both the score and the audio over a separate LM plus diffusion-Transformer cascade. This favors "score as an internal planning step in one model" over "two separately built layers."
2. **Audio-only control is now strong.** Adapters on audio models now give large control gains without a symbolic layer, mostly preserving quality. DiffSynth-Music raised Beat-F1 from 0.30 to 0.85 and Pitch-50 from 0.04 to 0.47. Its controls are beats, vocals, accompaniment and prosody, not melody, chords or key. An unmodified streaming audio model (Lyria RealTime), steered only by prompts, reportedly produced more off-grid quartertone maqam content.
3. **A discrete note layer is the wrong control point for some traditions.** The Hindustani work GaMaDHaNi drops note symbols on purpose. It models a fine pitch contour (10-cent bins) directly, saying prescriptive notation "fails to faithfully capture the fine melodic intricacies connecting these notes." Ethnomusicology has argued since the 1970s that staff-like categories impose European structure. The JRMA article cited below adds that even flexible notations "put in place certain categorical premisses."

So the defensible version is narrower. An explicit, editable score/plan representation helps control. For contour-centric traditions, the control layer may need to be the contour itself, or a culture-specific symbolic vocabulary (as in PerFormer for Persian music), not Western-style notes. Whether the layers should be separately trained stages or one jointly trained model remains open, and 2026 evidence (YuE2) leans joint.

Context, which weakens the case on both sides: the NAACL 2025 paper "Music for All" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07328, Mehta, Xia, Choudhury et al.) finds only 5.7% of music-dataset hours are non-Western. PEFT on MusicGen and Mustango for Hindustani and makam is "promising but non-trivial" (+8% and +4% Elo). End-to-end audio adaptation works only modestly. This shows neither architecture is proven for these traditions.

## Searched, not found

- No controlled head-to-head comparison of symbolic-then-render vs end-to-end audio generation on the same data, measuring both control adherence and quality, for any tradition.
- No quantified cascade-error study between a symbolic or semantic stage and audio rendering. Open-Qwen-Music (arXiv 2609.31652, 2026-09) only says its release enables this analysis. Its abstract reports no numbers.
- No end-to-end audio model with explicit, evaluated **key** or **tempo** control in 2025–2026 results. Melody and chord control exists (JASCO, MuseControlLite, MusiConGen). DiffSynth-Music covers beat and prosody, not chords or key.
- No published failure case or post-mortem of a two-stage symbolic-to-render system for non-Western singing, such as Taiwanese opera, Peking opera, Carnatic or makam vocals.
- No MOS or naturalness benchmark for MIDI- or score-driven Chinese-opera or Taiwanese-opera synthesis vs end-to-end audio.
- No Taiwanese opera (gezaixi) generation work of either type turned up, apart from the Gezi Opera FT-GAN mention (AAAI), which I did not verify in detail.
- No computational-ethnomusicology source arguing specifically that **note skeleton plus continuous contour** (as opposed to staff notation alone) misrepresents a tradition. The critiques found target staff notation and discrete categories in general.
