## Findings

### Q1: How existing ability tests build their stimuli
- The Melodic Discrimination Test (MDT) uses **automatic item generation (AIG)**. Base melodies come from a **Markov-chain model (Racchman-Jun2015)** trained on Irish folk melodies in triple time from the Essen collection. No more than 2 consecutive notes may come from the same source melody. In a 20-person pilot, generated melodies did not differ from the real source extracts in rated stylistic success (p = 0.48). This is rule-based, symbolic generation, not deep learning. | Applying modern psychometric techniques to melodic discrimination testing: IRT, CAT and AIG | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5472621 (read via Europe PMC full-text XML) | Scientific Reports (Nature) | 2017-06 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method | peer-reviewed (Sci Rep, 2017)
- MDT controlled variables: the "radicals" that drive difficulty are **melody length** (3 to 16 notes), **contour violation** and **tonality violation**. Each altered melody changes exactly 1 note, by at most 6 semitones. These give 20 item families in v1 and 32 in v2. The "incidentals" (base melody, odd-one-out position) vary at random. Idiom and transposition are held constant. Audio is MIDI rendered with a piano timbre at 120 bpm, with 1 s gaps between melodies. | same | same | Scientific Reports | 2017-06 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method | peer-reviewed
- The Computerised Adaptive Beat Alignment Test (CA-BAT; Harrison & Müllensiefen 2018) also combines IRT, CAT and AIG. It is reliable and valid in the lab but reported as poorly suited to online testing because it needs fine perceptual discrimination. | Development and Validation of the CA-BAT | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6097996/ | Frontiers in Psychology (per PMC; venue not verified this run) | 2018 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet only) | method | peer-reviewed
- The Mistuning Perception Test (MPT; Larrouy-Maestri, Harrison & Müllensiefen 2019) is adaptive and uses vocal (sung) stimuli, calibrated with explanatory item response modelling. Validation used N = 66. It ships as a psychTestR package (`mpt`, with `psychTestRCAT`). | The mistuning perception test: A new measurement instrument | https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-019-01225-1 ; https://rdrr.io/github/pmcharrison/mpt/f/README.md | Behavior Research Methods (Springer) | 2019 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet only) | method | peer-reviewed (BRM, 2019)
- PROMS (Law & Zentner 2012) has 9 same/different subtests: melody, pitch, timbre, tuning, rhythm, embedded rhythm, metric accent, tempo, loudness. Short-, Mini- and Micro-PROMS versions exist (the Micro version takes 10 min or less). The official guide calls the stimuli "culture-fair". **How the stimuli were built was not retrieved.** | Assessing Musical Abilities Objectively: Construction and Validation of PROMS ; PROMS User Guide | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3532219/ ; https://musemap.org/resources/proms | PLoS One / musemap.org | 2012 / guide 2025-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet only) | method | peer-reviewed (PLoS One, 2012)
- I found **no evidence that any established music-ability test uses generative-AI (deep-model) items.** The AIG that does exist (MDT, CA-BAT) is rule-based or Markov-chain. | (absence across searches above) | - | - | - | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | availability

### Q2: Deep generative models as experimental stimuli (2022 to 2026)
- Most recent studies that use AI music as stimuli test **attitudes and provenance bias** (does a "human" vs "AI" label change responses?). They do not use deep models to manipulate controlled variables. Examples:
  - PLOS One audiovisual study, N = 88: AI music from simple and detailed prompts; it produced greater pupil dilation and was rated more exciting, while human music felt more familiar.
  - Narrative-listening study (Cognitive Research: Principles & Implications, 2026): an AI label reduced imagined narratives, even when the music was actually human-made.
  - arXiv 2512.02785: attitude toward AI was the best predictor of liking.
  - 2026 detection study, N = 120: Suno and Udio outputs against commercial tracks.

### Q4: Evaluating generated-music quality and controllability
- The ACM Computing Surveys review (Lerch, Arthur, Bryan-Kinns et al.) covers evaluation targets, methods and metrics across subjective vs objective, qualitative vs quantitative, and empirical vs computational. | Survey on the Evaluation of Generative Models in Music | https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05104 | arXiv; listed as accepted at ACM CSUR | 2025-06 (v4 2025-09) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (existence) | method | peer-reviewed (accepted ACM CSUR, per arXiv comment)
- Common practice: MOS and head-to-head listening tests, and Turing-style human-vs-AI tests (critiqued for rewarding imitation over creativity). There is **no standardized subjective protocol**. Symbolic output must be synthesized to audio, which is itself a perceptual confound. | A Comprehensive Survey for Evaluation Methodologies of AI-Generated Music | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.13736 | arXiv | 2023-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet) | method | **preprint**
- Objective metrics correlate weakly with listening tests. ### Q5: Generated items combined with IRT difficulty modelling
- **Yes, a precedent exists, and it is pre-deep-learning.** The MDT uses explanatory IRT:
  - Model: a constrained 3PL with guessing = 1/3, inattention = 1 and a shared discrimination of 1.31.
  - Identical Siblings Model: items in a family share one difficulty.
  - Item difficulty regressed on the radicals gives adjusted R² = 0.869. Longer melodies are harder; contour and especially tonality violations make items easier.
  - Calibration: 1,200 items, 158 adults and 266 children.
  - Reliability: test-retest r = 0.67 at 11 items. Convergent validity: r = 0.53 with the Musical Ear Test (MET) and r = 0.44 with musical training.

  This is the template for "generate items with known features, then predict difficulty". | Harrison et al. 2017 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5472621 | Sci Rep | 2017-06 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method/benchmark | peer-reviewed
- The same IRT+CAT+AIG approach appears in CA-BAT and, through explanatory IRT, in the MPT. All are built on psychTestR/psychTestRCAT. | CA-BAT, MPT sources above | see above | - | 2018-2019 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | method | peer-reviewed
- I found **no study that combines deep-generative (neural) items with IRT difficulty calibration in music.** | (absence) | - | - | - | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | availability

## Leads
- **Main gap:** IRT+AIG music tests exist (MDT, CA-BAT, MPT). Deep generative stimuli exist, but only in attitude and bias studies. **Nobody evidenced this run bridges the two.** Next round: search for any 2024 to 2026 attempt directly (queries such as "neural item generation music test" or "LLM item generation auditory test").
- Read the rest of the MDT paper (Europe PMC XML, offset 100000) for the discussion of limitations and AIG.
- Other tests from the same group not yet checked: the Mistuning test's source stimuli, the Gold-MSI Beat Perception and Melody Memory subtests, the Pitch Imagery Arrow Task (PIAT), and the newer psychTestR tests (e.g., the Jack & Jill / SAA singing tests). Also check whether Gold-MSI test items are generated or hand-made.
- PROMS calls itself "culture-free", while Jacoby et al. show culture-specific rhythm priors. **This contradiction is worth chasing:** has PROMS been validated outside Europe?
- Jacoby et al.'s **iterated-reproduction / "serial reproduction" paradigm** is itself an algorithmic way to generate culturally grounded stimuli. Look at the Computational Auditory Perception group's PsyNet tooling.
- Harrison's later work (e.g., Mistuning, and the Long Tone / Melody singing tests) and the Mullensiefen group's "AIG" follow-ups after 2020.
- Read the Lerch et al. CSUR survey for its recommendations on controllability metrics (attribute-control accuracy, style classifiers).
- Verify the PMC12194076 and PMC12953833 journal identities and methods (both are AI-stimulus studies).
