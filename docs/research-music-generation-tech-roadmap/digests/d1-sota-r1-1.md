Accessed 2026-11-19. Budget: 13 tool calls. Two primary pages were read in full: the MuScriptor paper (via the alphaXiv mirror of arXiv 2607.08168v2) and the ACE-Step 1.5 arXiv abstract page. Every other row comes from search-result snippets of primary pages (arXiv, Hugging Face model cards, GitHub), so its confidence is capped at medium. ## Findings

### Q1 Automatic music transcription (AMT)
- MuScriptor is an open-weight multi-instrument transcription model (Rouard, Krause, Roebel, Simon-Gabriel, Défossez). It comes in 60M, 100M, 300M and 1.3B sizes. It is a decoder-only Transformer that reads 5 s mel windows plus an optional list of instruments and outputs MIDI-like tokens (pitch, timing, instrument). Training ran in three stages: synthetic pre-training on about 1.45M MIDI-rendered clips, fine-tuning on about 170k real recordings (over 11k h), and GRPO-style RL on 300 curated tracks. The paper says the weights and inference code are on github.com/muscriptor/muscriptor, but the paper text states **no licence for the code or weights**. | MuScriptor: An Open Model for Multi-Instrument Music Transcription | https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08168.md (arXiv 2607.08168v2) | arXiv / alphaXiv mirror | 2026-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (capability), low (licence) | capability/availability | **preprint**, listed for ISMIR 2026. Peer-review acceptance not verified.
- On MuScriptor's own test set D_Test, MuScriptor 1.3B scores onset F1 60.4 / frame 73.3 / offset 49.0 / drums 50.2 / multi-instrument 48.2. YourMT3+ on the same set scores 32.5 / 45.5 / 17.8 / 41.4 / 21.9. Per dataset the picture is mixed: YourMT3+ has higher onset F1 on Bach10 (59.8 vs 43.1), Dagstuhl ChoirSet (22.3 vs 14.4) and RWC-Classical. Choral singing is weak for both models (multi F1 2.6 vs 11.5). The paper reports no results on Slakh, MAESTRO or URMP. | same | same | arXiv / alphaXiv mirror | 2026-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (single source, competitor's own table) | benchmark | preprint
- YourMT3+ (QMUL) extends MT3 with hierarchical attention, mixture of experts, a multi-channel decoder and cross-dataset stem augmentation. It covers multi-instrument transcription including singing, outputs MIDI, has code on GitHub (mimbres/YourMT3) and a `yourmt3` package on PyPI. Its licence was not found. | YourMT3+: Multi-instrument Music Transcription… | https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04822v3 | arXiv / IEEE MLSP 2024 | 2024-07 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | capability/availability | peer-reviewed (IEEE MLSP 2024, per the QMUL publication PDF). By the freshness bar this is historical.
- Piano: Transkun (Yan & Duan) uses a non-hierarchical transformer with interval scoring. It reports state-of-the-art F1 across all MAESTRO subtasks, and its code is public (github.com/Yujia-Yan/Transkun). A transformer piano system from another 2024 paper (Toyama et al.) is reported at note F1 97.4 on MAESTRO, but that figure is self-reported and the source was not opened. Robustness is a known gap: Edwards et al. 2024 show piano AMT models overfit to the acoustics of their training data. | Scoring Intervals using Non-Hierarchical Transformer for Automatic Piano Transcription; A Data-Driven Analysis of Robust Automatic Piano Transcription | https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09466v6 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01424 | arXiv (Transkun: ISMIR 2024) | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | benchmark | Transkun peer-reviewed (ISMIR 2024) per the search snippet. Robust-AMT status unverified. No 2025–2026 piano state-of-the-art paper was found, so these are historical.
- Basic Pitch (Spotify) is a lightweight audio-to-MIDI model for many instruments, released with an ICASSP 2022 paper. It is Apache-2.0 (GitHub repo and HF model card), with Python and TS/JS versions. It outputs MIDI only, not a score. | spotify/basic-pitch | https://github.com/spotify/basic-pitch | Spotify | 2022 (live repo) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (licence: repo plus HF card agree) | licence/availability | ICASSP 2022 peer-reviewed. Historical by accuracy.
- Singing voice: no 2025–2026 benchmark leader was established. Candidates are ROSVOT (ZJU, 2024, note-level, code on GitHub, reports about 17% relative pitch improvement over the previous best), a ByteDance Mel-RoFormer vocal-melody transcriber (2025, ISMIR proceedings on Zenodo, claims state of the art and robust offsets, implementation referenced) and SongTrans (2024, joint lyrics plus notes). | Robust Singing Voice Transcription Serves Synthesis; Mel-RoFormer…; SongTrans | https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09940v2 ; https://zenodo.org/record/14877371/files/000049.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.14619 | arXiv / ISMIR | 2024–2025 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low–medium (snippets only, no F1 tables read) | capability | ROSVOT: ACL 2024 (unverified). Mel-RoFormer: ISMIR 2025 (unverified from snippet).
- Audio to **score** (MusicXML), not just MIDI, is still mostly a two-stage process: audio→MIDI, then MIDI→score. Beyer & Dai (ISMIR 2024) convert piano performance MIDI to MusicXML end to end. Zeng et al. (IJCAI 2024) do end-to-end piano audio→score with bar-level key and time signatures. No general multi-instrument audio→MusicXML model was found. | End-to-End Piano Performance-MIDI to Score Conversion with Transformers; IJCAI 2024 862 | https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00210v1 ; https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/862 | ISMIR / IJCAI | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | capability | peer-reviewed (ISMIR 2024; IJCAI 2024)

### Q2 Expressive performance rendering (score→performance)
- SyMuPe / PianoFlow (Borovik et al.) is a framework that tokenises score and performance MIDI and uses flow matching for rendering. It can be steered by emotion text embeddings, and a `symupe` PyPI package ships rendering and score-performance alignment code. The paper itself notes that models do not yet support real-time interactive inference and that the field is moving toward audio rendering. | SyMuPe: Affective and Controllable Symbolic Music Performance | https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03425v1 ; https://pypi.org/project/symupe/ | arXiv / ACM MM 2025 | 2025-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | capability/availability | peer-reviewed ACM MM '25 per the search snippet (unverified). Licence not checked.
- Other 2025–2026 rendering work, openness unverified: Peransformer (APSIPA 2025, score-aware discriminator), joint rendering plus transcription with disentangled style (arXiv 2509.23878), Pianist Transformer (self-supervised on unlabelled MIDI, arXiv 2512.02652), PianoKontext (arXiv 2606.12282), MIDI-VALLE (expressive piano synthesis via codec LM, arXiv 2507.08530), a score→expressive MIDI→neural synth audio pipeline (arXiv 2501.10222) and RenderBox (text plus score→multi-instrument audio, arXiv 2502.07711). All are piano-centric except RenderBox. | search snippets | https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23878v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02652v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2502.07711v1 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10222v1 | arXiv | 2025–2026 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | capability | mostly preprints

### Q3 Controllable symbolic generation
- The Anticipatory Music Transformer (Stanford CRFM) is controllable by infilling, which covers accompaniment of a given melody and the converse. Its weights are on HF (small 128M, large 780M). HF cards and the repo are **Apache-2.0**. Training data includes Lakh MIDI, and the large card also cites transcripts from FMA and about 450k commercial records. The paper discusses copyright of training data in its appendix, so the commercial position needs checking. | Anticipatory Music Transformer; stanford-crfm/music-large-800k card | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.08620 ; https://huggingface.co/stanford-crfm/music-large-800k | Stanford CRFM | 2023 (paper), cards live | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (licence: HF cards plus repo, both from snippets) | licence/capability | TMLR 2024 (unverified). Seminal but older.
- Moonbeam (May 2025) is a symbolic foundation model supporting conditional generation and infilling. Code, pretrained model and samples are open-sourced; licence not checked. | Moonbeam | https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15559 | arXiv | 2025-05 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low–medium | availability | preprint
- MIDI-RWKV (RWKV-7, adaptable infilling, 2025-06, revised 2026-01) and MIDI-GPT (track- and bar-level infilling with attribute conditioning) were found. Licence and weights are unverified for both. | Adaptable Symbolic Music Infilling with MIDI-RWKV; MIDI-GPT | https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13001v2 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.17011v2 | arXiv | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | capability | preprints (MIDI-GPT venue unverified)
- Text-to-MIDI: Text2midi (AMAAI Lab, 272M) is Apache-2.0 on HF, trained on MidiCaps, with captions covering genre, mood, key, tempo and instruments. MIDI-LLM (Llama-3.2-1B based, Nov 2025) has open code and weights on HF. Its authors claim better quality and text control than Text2midi; this is self-reported. | Text2midi; MIDI-LLM | https://huggingface.co/amaai-lab/text2midi ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03942v1 | AMAAI / arXiv | 2024-12 / 2025-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (Text2midi licence from HF card), low (MIDI-LLM licence) | licence/capability | Text2midi peer-reviewed AAAI 2025. ### Q4 Audio-domain generators
- ACE-Step 1.5 (arXiv 2602.00744, Jan–Feb 2026) uses an LM planner to produce metadata, lyrics and captions, plus a DiT. It offers stylistic control, 50+ languages, lengths up to 10 min, cover generation, repainting, vocal→BGM (accompaniment for a vocal) and LoRA personalisation from a few songs. The abstract does not mention key/scale, BPM, stem or track control, or any MIDI/score output. | ACE-Step 1.5: Pushing the Boundaries of Open-Source Music Generation | https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00744v2 | arXiv | 2026-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (as stated in the abstract) | capability | **preprint**
- ACE-Step 1.5 licence: the repo README and HF base model card say **MIT**. An open HF discussion asks maintainers to confirm that MIT covers the weights and the bundled VAE, and no resolution was found. Treat the weights licence as medium-confidence MIT until the live LICENSE file is checked. | ACE-Step/acestep-v15-base README; HF discussion #27 | https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/acestep-v15-base/blob/main/README.md ; https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5/discussions/27 | ACE-Step / HF | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | licence

## Leads
1. **Contradiction to resolve:** YourMT3+ beats MuScriptor on onset F1 on Bach10, ChoirSet and RWC-Classical, but loses badly on MuScriptor's own D_Test. Both sets of numbers come from MuScriptor's table. YourMT3+'s own paper figures on Slakh, MAESTRO and URMP need reading, and MuScriptor needs an independent evaluation.
2. MuScriptor's code and weights licence: check the LICENSE file at github.com/muscriptor/muscriptor (this is a decision-critical gap). Also find out whether it emits instrument program numbers usable for scoring.
3. YourMT3 licence (mimbres/YourMT3 LICENSE), Transkun licence and SyMuPe licence and weights.
4. ACE-Step 1.5 weights licence: is HF discussion #27 resolved? Check the full paper for key, BPM, timbre-reference and stem controls.
5. Check the other audio generators this round did not cover: MusicGen (CC-BY-NC weights, per prior belief only), Stable Audio Open (community licence, per prior belief only), YuE and any 2026 successors. Re-verify their live licences.
6. Singing transcription: read the F1 tables in the ROSVOT and Mel-RoFormer (ISMIR 2025) papers and check whether the Mel-RoFormer weights are released.
7. Style or composer conditioning in symbolic models, e.g. composer tokens in Moonbeam or MIDI-GPT attributes, and models trained on classical corpora such as Aria (EleutherAI) or the GiantMIDI-based ones. Not searched this round.
8. Audio→MusicXML: is the Beyer & Dai MIDI→MusicXML model released, and under what licence? Also check MuseScore or other tools for score import from MIDI.
