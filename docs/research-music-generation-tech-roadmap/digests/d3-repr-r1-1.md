### Q1. Limits of MIDI and staff notation, and how current standards handle them
- MIDI 2.0 carries per-note pitch through Note On attributes and per-note pitch controllers. Pitch Bend is 32-bit, so microtonal per-note pitch is possible at spec level. Under default MIDI 1.0 to 2.0 translation the attribute type is "none" unless a MIDI-CI Profile sets it. | The state of MIDI 2.0: high-resolution performance and the rise of profiles (update Feb 2026) / midi.org quick reference | https://midi.org/the-state-of-midi-2-0-high-resolution-performance-and-the-rise-of-profiles-update-feb-2026 ; https://midi.org/?p=6489 | MIDI Association | 2026-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (seen through search snippets; spec PDF not read) | capability | standards body
- The "7.9 fixed-point pitch" format of the per-note pitch attribute (7-bit semitone plus 9-bit fraction, about 0.2 cent) is reported only by forum users. I did not check it against the spec. Users also found no confirmed list of synths or players that honour arbitrary per-note pitch, so real implementation support is unproven. | Are there more than 128 notes in MIDI 2.0? | https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8160672 | KVR Audio forum | n/a | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | availability | forum, not authoritative
- MusicXML handles quarter tones natively: accidental values quarter-flat/sharp and three-quarters-flat/sharp, plus a numeric decimal `<alter>` (e.g. 0.5). Finer divisions such as 1/12-tone and HEJI go through SMuFL glyph attributes (supported "as of MusicXML 3.1"). The accidental names do not define exact tunings. | W3C public-music-notation-contrib posts (M. Good 2016; 2020 thread) | https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-music-notation-contrib/2016Jul/0007.html ; https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-music-notation-contrib/2020May/0008.html | W3C Music Notation CG | 2016-07 / 2020-05 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | capability | mailing list of spec maintainers
- In practice MusicXML round-trips lose microtonal accidentals. Dorico reads them but does not export them, and OpenMusic 8 import ignores them. | Microtonality and MusicXML; IRCAM forum thread | https://forums.steinberg.net/t/microtonality-and-musicxml/863784 ; https://discussion.forum.ircam.fr/t/musicxml-import-still-ignores-quarter-tone-accidentals-in-openmusic-8-0/183126 | Steinberg / IRCAM forums | n/a | accessed 2026-11-19 | low-medium | availability | forum
- MEI separates the written accidental (`@accid`) from the sounding one (`@accid.ges`). Quarter-tone values (`qf`, `qs`) exist. I found no cents-valued attribute. | MEI Guidelines v5, `accid`; MEI-L "Encoding quarter tones" | https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/accid.html ; https://lists.uni-paderborn.de/pipermail/mei-l/2016/001858.html | Music Encoding Initiative | v5 / 2016 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | capability | standards docs
- Conceptual limit: the VocalNotes project asks whether the "note" (onset, duration, pitch) is the right unit for many traditions. It also asks whether ornaments are separate notes or part of the note they decorate. This bears directly on glides, ornaments and heterophony. | VocalNotes (IFTAWM journal) | https://journal.iftawm.org/?p=4218 | IFTAWM journal | unknown | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (snippet only) | method | journal; peer-review status unverified

### Q2. Symbolic representations for non-Western music in ML
- SymbTr (Turkish makam): about 3,000 pieces, 164 makams and roughly 1.2M notes in v3.0, released as text, MusicXML, PDF, MIDI and mu2. Licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which blocks commercial use. Older releases have about 2,200 pieces and 155 makams (Karaosmanoğlu, ISMIR 2012). A phrase dataset adds 31,362 expert-annotated phrases over 480 scores. | SymbTr on Zenodo / GitHub MTG/symbtr | https://zenodo.org/records/15470412 ; https://github.com/MTG/symbtr | UPF-MTG (CompMusic) | 2016-10 (v3 record) | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (licence comes from a snippet; live licence page not read; one source) | dataset-size, licence | ISMIR 2012 paper, peer-reviewed
- Gongche (工尺譜) OMR: Chen and Sheu (2014) built an OMR system for handwritten Kunqu gongche scores and evaluated it on only 39 images. A 2021 deep-learning follow-up and a 2024 algorithm comparison exist. A 2024 chapter says Chinese traditional-music OMR "is still limited". I found no public gongche OMR dataset. | An optical music recognition system for traditional Chinese Kunqu Opera scores written in Gong-Che notation | https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/1687-4722-2014-7 ; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-70552-6_3 | EURASIP J. Audio Speech Music Proc.; Springer | 2014; 2024 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | dataset-size, availability | peer-reviewed (EURASIP JASMP 2014)
- Suzipu/banzipu (Song-dynasty notation, KuiSCIMA): Repolusk and Veas released a public dataset. Search snippets name it as the exception among East-Asian OMR work. | Proceedings of the 6th International Workshop on Reading Music Systems | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15741 | WoRMS | 2024-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | dataset | workshop proceedings
- Jianpu (簡譜) OMR: large annotated jianpu-plus-lyrics datasets are rare. Earlier attempts used synthetic or rendered scores. A Dec 2025 pipeline uses mostly classical computer vision ("expert systems") so it needs no large training set. Other work: an MDPI multi-font CNN dataset (Electronics 11(21):3592, 2022) and Wu (2017, IJMDEM). | The Renaissance of Expert Systems: Optical Recognition of Printed Chinese Jianpu Musical Scores with Lyrics | https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14758v1 | arXiv | 2025-12 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | availability | preprint
- Jingju scores: the Jingju Music Scores Collection has 92 machine-readable scores covering 897 melodic lines. These are Western-staff transcriptions, which is a fact about the corpus and not a claim that staff suffices. | Annotated jingju arias / CompMusic jingju resources | https://compmusic.upf.edu/zh-hans/node/349 | UPF-MTG | ~2017 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | dataset-size | dataset page

### Q3. MIR on Chinese opera (jingju, Gezaixi)
- The jingju a cappella singing dataset (ISMIR 2017) covers 120 arias and 1,265 melodic lines and is openly available. A related ground-truth release (Gong, Yang and Serra, SMC 2016) provides pYIN f0 tracks, note transcriptions and segmentation. Audio is held back for size and available on request. Jingju MIR therefore pairs continuous f0 with note-level annotation, as in a hybrid. | Jingju a cappella singing dataset (ISMIR 2017); Zenodo pitch-contour segmentation ground truth | https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2017/paper/000119.pdf ; https://zenodo.org/records/832736 | ISMIR / UPF | 2017-07 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium-high | dataset-size, method | peer-reviewed (ISMIR 2017; SMC 2016)
- Gezaixi/Kua-á-hì: the only MIR work I found builds vocal segments plus lyrics from archival TV. It separates singing from speech by OCR subtitle display duration and targets lyrics identification and tune (曲調) retrieval. It provides no pitch or score representation, and no dataset size is stated. Code is at z-huang/ocr-subtitle-editor. Release of the dataset itself is not stated. | Timed text extraction from Taiwanese Kua-á-hì TV series (Huang, Tsai, Hung, Wu, Wei, Su) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00299v1 | arXiv (ISMIR 2025 LBD) | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (for what it states) | dataset, availability | late-breaking demo, not full peer review

### Q4. Continuous pitch vs discrete notes in computational ethnomusicology
- Tzanetakis (ICMC-SMC 2014) frames computational ethnomusicology as part of MIR. He notes that pitch discretisation is a cultural or instrument convention. Earlier systems (ISMIR 2006, Tarsos) start from continuous pitch and avoid a priori pitch categories. | Computational ethnomusicology (ICMC-SMC 2014) | https://speech.di.uoa.gr/ICMC-SMC-2014/images/VOL_1/0069.pdf | ICMC-SMC | 2014 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet) | method | peer-reviewed conference; historical
- Learned discrete contour tokens are an emerging hybrid. A VQ-VAE tokenises pitch-contour segments of Korean sigimsae ornaments into a learned vocabulary. The authors argue predefined note or ornament categories depend on manual labour. | Pitch Contour Tokenization using VQ-VAE and Its Application on Korean Traditional Music Analysis | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10979 | arXiv | 2026-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet) | method | preprint
- Stabilised-pitch-contour distributions beat note-level features for classifying Ethiopian Orthodox chant modes (silt). | arXiv 2412.18788 | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18788 | arXiv | 2024-12 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (snippet, single source) | benchmark | preprint
- Oral-tradition tunings can be derived from f0 histograms aligned to MIDI by DTW. | arXiv 2503.11956 | https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11956 | arXiv | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | method | preprint

### Q5. Generative models for non-12-TET music
- GaMaDHaNi (Hindustani vocal) is a two-level hierarchy: a generator over a finely quantised pitch contour feeds a contour-to-audio synthesiser conditioned on singer. The contour is modelled both as discrete tokens (autoregressive transformer) and as continuous values (diffusion). It produces a mel-spectrogram, then audio via Griffin-Lim. The authors explicitly reject "coarse discrete symbols" for melody and argue the contour layer enables primed generation and coarse pitch conditioning for human-AI interaction. Paper licence is CC BY 4.0; no code link appears on the abstract page. | Hierarchical Generative Modeling of Melodic Vocal Contours in Hindustani Classical Music (Shikarpur et al.) | https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12658v2 | ISMIR 2024 | 2024-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (architecture); historical (over 6 months old) | method, capability | peer-reviewed (ISMIR 2024)
- A follow-up human-AI interaction study with Hindustani musicians uses the same system. | Exploratory Study of Human-AI Interaction for Hindustani Music | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13846 | arXiv | 2024-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (unread) | lead | preprint (status unverified)

## Leads
1. **Contradiction or tension:** MIDI 2.0 can express per-note microtonal pitch on paper, but no implementer list confirms it works in practice. Next, read the official UMP spec (M2-104) and the MIDI Association "Note On pitch 7.9" profile, and check MPE (MIDI 1.0 per-channel bend) support in synths and DAWs.
2. GaMaDHaNi's contour-as-intermediate design is the clearest template for a "score layer to performance layer" split in contour-centric traditions. Check whether code and data have been released, and whether it has been ported to Carnatic, jingju or Gezaixi.
3. Find Carnatic gamaka-specific representations: the "compact gamaka representation" and "non-uniform pitch quantisation preserving raga characteristics" papers cited by GaMaDHaNi.
4. Read the VQ-VAE contour tokenisation paper (2608.10979) in full as a candidate tokeniser for 歌仔戲 and jingju glides. It is a preprint.
5. Look for jingju or Kunqu generative models and singing-voice synthesis. Also look for Chinese opera SVS datasets such as Opencpop-style corpora for 戲曲.
6. Check whether the Kua-á-hì dataset will be released; Li Su's lab at Academia Sinica is a likely partner for Gezaixi data.
7. Look into Japanese traditional notation (shakuhachi honkyoku, gagaku, Noh) encoding and datasets, and minority-music (e.g. Taiwanese Indigenous polyphony) corpora. Neither was searched this round.
8. Look for representations of heterophony and improvisation: multi-stream contour alignment, for example in jingju jinghu versus voice. Not searched.
9. Re-check the SymbTr licence (CC BY-NC-SA) on the live GitHub LICENSE file before relying on it.

## Looked for, not found
- No authoritative list of synths or players implementing MIDI 2.0 per-note pitch attributes.
- No cents-valued pitch attribute in MEI; only quarter-tone `accid.ges` values were found.
- No public gongche OMR dataset; the main system was evaluated on 39 images.
- No standard public jianpu OMR benchmark.
- No pitch-contour or score-level MIR study of Gezaixi singing; the only item is a lyrics and audio segmentation LBD.
- No Carnatic generative model under the name GaMaDHaNi; it covers Hindustani only.
- MPE, improvisation and heterophony representations, and Japanese and minority musics were not reached within budget.
