## Findings

### Q1: Standard recipe, tokenizers, pretrained models, licences
- The current MidiTok release is 3.1.0 (released 2026-09-30) under the MIT licence. It supports REMI, REMI+, MIDI-Like, TSD, BEAT, Structured, CPWord (Compound Word), Octuple, MuMIDI, MMM and PerTok. Tokenizers can be pushed to and loaded from the Hugging Face Hub (`push_to_hub` / `from_pretrained`). | miditok on PyPI | https://pypi.org/project/miditok/ | PyPI | 2026-09 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | availability/licence | n/a
- Anticipatory Music Transformer: code is Apache-2.0. Pretrained checkpoints are hosted by Stanford CRFM on Hugging Face (e.g. `stanford-crfm/music-medium-800k`). The README shows accompaniment from a melody out of the box: extract the melody instrument, pass it as `controls=` to `generate()`, then combine. Training uses Levanter (JAX). The README has no fine-tuning instructions. | jthickstun/anticipation README | https://github.com/jthickstun/anticipation | GitHub (Stanford) | n.d. (live page) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | licence/availability/method | n/a
- Paper: anticipatory infilling models match autoregressive models on prompted generation and also support infilling and accompaniment. Listeners rated 20-second accompaniments as similar in musicality to human-composed music. Trained on Lakh MIDI. | Anticipatory Music Transformer | https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08620 | arXiv / TMLR | 2023-06 (TMLR accepted version) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (one source) | method/benchmark | peer-reviewed (TMLR, per arXiv comment)
- Aria (EleutherAI): code and weights are Apache-2.0. It uses the LLaMA 3.2 1B architecture and was trained on about 60k hours of transcribed expressive solo piano (Aria-MIDI dataset on HF). Released checkpoints are aria-medium-gen (continuation) and aria-medium-embedding. Inference runs on CUDA and on MLX (Apple Silicon). The README gives no fine-tuning instructions or training hardware. Piano only, performance-style (expressive) MIDI. | EleutherAI/aria README | https://github.com/EleutherAI/aria | GitHub | n.d. (live page) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | licence/availability | paper arXiv 2506.23869: README title says ISMIR 2025, BibTeX says preprint. Status unconfirmed.
- NotaGen: the repo is MIT and no separate weight licence is stated. It works in ABC notation. Pretrained sizes are 110M, 244M and 516M. It was pretrained on 1.6M ABC pieces and fine-tuned on about 9K classical works with "period-composer-instrumentation" prompts. The pretrained (not fine-tuned) weights cannot do prompted generation. CLaMP-DPO is an RL step that needs no human labels. | NotaGen repo; NotaGen paper | https://github.com/ElectricAlexis/NotaGen ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18008 | GitHub / arXiv | 2025-02 (v5 2025-03) | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (licence: medium, weights unstated) | licence/method/dataset-size | preprint (no venue on arXiv page)
- MIDI-GPT (Metacreation/SFU + Steinberg) is a multitrack model of about 20M parameters (GPT-2, 6 layers, 2048-token context) trained on GigaMIDI. It supports track-level infilling, bar-level infilling, and attribute controls (instrument, style, note density, polyphony, note duration), with optional velocity and microtiming. Weights are under Open RAIL-M (use-restricted, not OSI). Its tokenization (MMM) ships in MidiTok. | MIDI-GPT | https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17011v2 | arXiv | 2025-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method/licence | preprint
- Synthesis (inference, not a single-source fact): the 2025–26 practical recipe is a MidiTok tokenizer (REMI-family or MMM for multitrack) plus a decoder-only transformer, or an ABC text model (NotaGen, ChatMusician-style LLMs). Pretrained open checkpoints now exist for each of the user's goals: AMT for accompaniment given a melody, Aria for expressive piano, NotaGen for composer/period-conditioned scores, MIDI-GPT for multitrack infilling plus attribute control. This favours adopting and fine-tuning over training from scratch. | (multiple above) | — | — | — | — | medium | method

### Q2: Compute reality
- MIDI-GPT (about 20M params) trains in "2-3 days using 4 V100 GPUs", roughly 200–300 V100-hours. This is the scale of from-scratch training for a small multitrack model. | MIDI-GPT | https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17011v2 | arXiv | 2025-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (one source) | compute | preprint
- NotaGen-large needs at least 24GB of GPU memory for training/fine-tuning and inference. Smaller variants (110M/244M) can be swapped in via `finetune/config.py`. Local NotaGen-X inference needs about 8GB. A 24GB consumer card (RTX 3090/4090) covers fine-tuning NotaGen-large; 8–16GB cards are limited to the smaller variants. | NotaGen repo | https://github.com/ElectricAlexis/NotaGen | GitHub | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | compute | n/a
- Aria runs on Apple Silicon via MLX for inference, which gives a no-cloud option for experiments on a Mac. | EleutherAI/aria | https://github.com/EleutherAI/aria | GitHub | n.d. | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | compute/availability | n/a

### Q3: General LLMs on ABC/symbolic music
- ABC-Eval (10 sub-tasks, 1,086 items) tested 7 LLMs. Overall averages: GPT-5 55.0%, GPT-5-mini 53.5%, DeepSeek-reasoner 52.0%, Gemini-2.5-pro 51.3%, GPT-5-nano 49.0%, Gemini-flash 44.1%, DeepSeek-chat 40.2%. These are averages over mixed metrics. "All foundation models failed" segment-level tasks (next-bar prediction, bar ordering, error detection). Emotion, composer and genre recognition were near chance (25%). Performance on bar counting was unstable across prompts. | ABC-Eval: Benchmarking LLMs on symbolic music | https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23350v1 | arXiv | 2025-09 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (one source; second-source corroboration pending) | benchmark | preprint. Over 6 months old, so historical.
- Fine-tuning LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct on ABC (SFT, DPO) improves music understanding, with SFT best. On generation, perplexity favoured SFT and ChatMusician while DPO got worse. FMD showed no clear trend. Domain gains came at the cost of general ability (MMLU), with ChatMusician losing most. The authors list compute and context-length limits as a block on multi-part scores. | How Far Can Pretrained LLMs Go in Symbolic Music? | https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22764v1 | arXiv | 2026-01 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | benchmark/method | preprint
- MSU-Bench (1,800 QA items, more than 15 models) reports a gap between ABC-text and PDF-score understanding. Known only from the search snippet; the paper was not read. | MSU-Bench | https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20697v2 | arXiv | 2025-11 (rev 2026-01) | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | benchmark | preprint
- Synthesis: the evidence shows general LLMs are weak at bar-level structural reasoning in ABC. They are usable for metadata and syntax-level work and natural-language front-ends, not as the core generator. | (above) | — | — | — | — | medium | capability

### Q4: Conditioning techniques
- Anticipation (interleaving control events such as a melody ahead of the generated stream) gives melody-to-accompaniment with an existing checkpoint, through a documented API. It is the lowest-effort route to goal (a). | AMT README + paper | https://github.com/jthickstun/anticipation | GitHub/TMLR | — | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method
- Prompt or control-token conditioning (period-composer-instrumentation) is how NotaGen does style/composer control. It requires fine-tuned weights; the pretrained weights do not respond. | NotaGen repo | https://github.com/ElectricAlexis/NotaGen | GitHub | — | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | method
- Attribute control tokens (density, polyphony, duration, instrument, style) plus track/bar infilling are implemented in MIDI-GPT, with tokenization in MidiTok. The authors report attribute control as effective for steering. | MIDI-GPT | https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17011v2 | arXiv | 2025-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | method | preprint
- Pitfall: naively fine-tuning a pretrained model on new fine-grained (bar-level) controls often fails to make it follow them. MuseBarControl adds a control-pretraining stage plus a counterfactual loss: chord accuracy 78.3% vs 65.3% for plain fine-tuning (built on MuseCoco). Known from the search snippet only. | MuseBarControl | https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04331 | arXiv | 2024-07 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low-medium | method/benchmark | preprint (status not checked)
- Inference-time activation steering for attribute control without retraining has been proposed for the Multitrack Music Transformer. Not verified. | Latent Space Disentanglement via Activation Steering | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.31295 | arXiv | 2026-05 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | method | preprint

### Q5: Pitfalls beyond monophony
- Copying in symbolic transformers stems from local overfitting to short recurring motifs: frequent motifs get lower perplexity and reappear in outputs. Tested mitigations are label smoothing, transposition augmentation and top-k sampling. | Why Do Music Models Plagiarize? A Motif-Centric Perspective | https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/123788 | NeurIPS 2025 (workshop per snippet) | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet only) | method/pitfall | workshop paper
- Melody-based plagiarism measures assume monophony and break on polyphony. SSIMuse adapts SSIM to piano rolls for training-free replication detection in polyphonic music. | SSIMuse | https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13658 | arXiv | 2025-09 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet) | pitfall/eval | preprint
- MIDI-GPT reports that duplication of training excerpts falls as generated length grows, and that infills of 4 or more bars are reliably original (Jaccard). Deployed products check for and regenerate infills identical to the source. | MIDI-GPT | https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17011v2 | arXiv | 2025-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (one source) | pitfall/eval | preprint
- Full-song multitrack work reports memorisation ratios as high as 55% on some checkpoints and none on others. A piano-transformer study found overfitting checkpoints sound more coherent while validation loss worsens, a warning that loss and "sounds good" can both hide memorisation. Both known from search snippets only. | PhraseVAE/PhraseLDM; arXiv 2511.07268 | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11348 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07268v2 | arXiv | 2025-12; 2025-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low-medium | pitfall | preprints
- Evaluation difficulty: FMD showed no clear trends and "may not correlate well with human judgments" (2601.22764). Human listening tests remain the reference (AMT, NotaGen A/B tests). | (above) | — | — | — | — | medium | eval

## Leads
- Contradiction: Aria's README title says ISMIR 2025 but its BibTeX says arXiv preprint. Check the ISMIR 2025 proceedings.
- NotaGen weights licence: the repo is MIT but the HF model cards may differ. Check HF cards. Also check its venue (possibly IJCAI 2025, unverified).
- AMT checkpoint sizes (small/medium/large) and parameter counts, and whether HF fine-tuning works on a 24GB GPU (Levanter is JAX/TPU-oriented). Check the HF model cards and the `train/` directory.
- ChatMusician and MuPT licences and sizes (not checked this round).
- Classifier-free guidance applied to symbolic transformers: no source found this round.
- MidiTok "attribute controls" feature (in 3.x?) as a ready-made control-token implementation. Check MidiTok docs.
- Free-tier cloud (Colab/Kaggle T4 16GB) feasibility for LoRA fine-tuning of NotaGen-small/medium or AMT-small.
- Second source for the ABC-Eval conclusions (MSU-Bench full paper; ACL 2026 paper aclanthology 2026.acl-long.493).
- Activation steering (2605.31295) is a no-retraining control option worth verifying for a low-compute user.
