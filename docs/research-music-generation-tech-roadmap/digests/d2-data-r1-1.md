### Q2 Paired score + performance/audio
- ASAP: 222 MusicXML/MIDI scores aligned with 1,067–1,068 MIDI performances (over 92 h) of Western classical piano. Annotations cover beats, downbeats, time signatures and keys. 519 performances have audio, which must be pulled from MAESTRO v2.0.0. Licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The README's own counts are inconsistent (222 vs 236 scores; 1,067 vs 1,068 performances). | ASAP dataset README | https://github.com/fosfrancesco/asap-dataset | F. Foscarin et al. (GitHub) | live | 2026-11-19 | high | dataset-size, licence
- MAESTRO (above) is the main audio↔performance-MIDI pair. ASAP adds the score layer on top of MAESTRO. Both are NC-SA, so any score→performance→audio pipeline trained on them inherits non-commercial terms (inference).

- Pattern across sources (synthesis): CompMusic non-Western corpora (jingju, makam, Indian) are consistently **NonCommercial**, sometimes NoDerivatives or restricted-access, and mostly **audio + annotations**. ### Q5 Licensing reality / legal developments
- GEMA v OpenAI, Munich Regional Court I, 11 Nov 2025 (42 O 14139/24): the court held that memorising lyrics in model weights and reproducing them in outputs infringes copyright. The TDM exception does not cover storing or outputting protected lyrics, and OpenAI's claim to research-organisation privilege was rejected. The judgment covers 9 songs, is not final, and is likely appealed. GEMA also has separate proceedings against Suno. | Taylor Wessing, Bird & Bird, GEMA notices | https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2025/11/gema-v-openai ; https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/landmark-ruling-of-the-munich-regional-court-(gema-v-openai)-on-copyright-and-ai-training ; https://www.gema.de/en/w/landmark-ruling-gema-against-openai | law firms + GEMA (independent publishers) | 2025-11 | 2026-11-19 | medium (via search snippets from several independent publishers; the current appeal status in Nov 2026 is NOT checked, so this is over 12 months old) | legal
- Implication for this project (inference): the main risks are memorisation and reproduction of protected material, not training as such. Corpora built from copyrighted repertoire carry risk if a model regurgitates them in stimuli (Lakh = pop transcriptions, Aria-MIDI/GiantMIDI = YouTube-derived performances). PDMX `no_license_conflict` is the cleanest large symbolic corpus. NC licences (MAESTRO, ASAP, Aria-MIDI, SymbTr, jingju, M4Singer) suit academic research-stimulus use but block commercial reuse of the trained model or outputs, depending on how "derivative" is read. That last point is legally unsettled.

## Leads
1. **Contradictions to resolve:** Opencpop licence (CC BY-NC-ND vs GPL-2.0 tag) needs a check against the official repo. The ASAP README's internal counts disagree. The Aria-MIDI licence is "other" on one mirror and CC BY-NC-SA on the HF card.
2. **Two-source rule still unmet** for most licence claims. Open the live LICENSE/record pages for: SymbTr LICENSE, Saraga Zenodo 1.5 licence field, jingju scores record, POP909 repo, Aria-MIDI HF card, GiantMIDI disclaimer.md.
3. Taiwanese opera: contact or follow up with Li Su's lab (Academia Sinica MCT Lab) on the Kua-á-hì dataset release. The FT-GAN Gezi Opera dataset needs its access terms found. Also check Taiwan's National Center for Traditional Arts (國立傳統藝術中心) and the Taiwan Music Institute for archive access.
4. GEMA v OpenAI appeal status (2026). GEMA v Suno outcome. Suno/Udio–major label settlements and licensing deals (2025). US Copyright Office Part 3 report on generative-AI training. EU AI Act GPAI training-data summary obligations (applying from Aug 2025). None of these were checked this run.
5. Newer large symbolic sets from 2025–2026 (for example any successors to Aria-MIDI or PDMX, MidiCaps, GigaMIDI). Not searched this run.
6. Aria-MIDI size: my belief is about 1M+ MIDI files / about 100k h. This is unverified and needs a fetch of the paper.
