# Digest B: prompt-copy / similarity / texture metrics (r1-1)

Scope: objective metrics to penalise a generated piano accompaniment that replays the block-chord conditioning prompt, while still allowing partial reuse. Accessed 2026-11-22. Tool budget ran out (12 calls). Two PDFs could not be parsed (no PDF renderer), so several formulas below come only from search-result summaries. Those are flagged.

## Findings

- **Grooving pattern similarity (GS)**: a bar's onset grid is a binary vector g ∈ {0,1}^64. GS(g_a, g_b) = 1 − (1/Q)·Σ_q XOR(g_a[q], g_b[q]), with Q = 64, so the range is [0,1]. Proposed as a within-piece rhythmicity measure. Applied as GS(candidate bar, block-chord prompt bar), it scores how closely the candidate's onsets match the prompt's. A block-chord prompt has onsets only at chord changes, so a high GS means the candidate copies the prompt's rhythm. | "The Jazz Transformer on the Front Line" (Wu & Yang) https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01307 | ISMIR 2020 (peer-reviewed; venue confirmed on the arXiv abs page) | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: medium (formula comes from the search snippet of the PDF; I could not read the PDF text) | class: metric-definition
  - Pitfall: XOR counts shared silences as matches. A very sparse candidate scored against a sparse block-chord prompt gets a high GS, and a dense random candidate gets a low GS (it looks "original"). Use it with an onset-density floor and ceiling. The arXiv page links no code: unverified whether official code exists.
- **GS variants**: MIDI-RWKV restates GS as the average share of onset positions (present or absent) that match, credited to Wu & Yang 2020. A 2026 piano-arrangement paper uses 4-bar segments at 1/4-beat resolution and cosine similarity instead of XOR, averaged over non-overlapping segments. Cosine ignores shared silences, which reduces the sparse-output pitfall. | "Adaptable Symbolic Music Infilling with MIDI-RWKV" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13001 ; "Learning Music Style for Piano Arrangement Through Cross-Modal Bootstrapping" https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03050 | arXiv preprints | 2025 / 2026 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: medium (from search summary) | class: metric-definition
- **mgeval feature set**: pitch count, pitch-class histogram, pitch-class transition matrix (PCTM), pitch range, average pitch interval, note count, average inter-onset interval (IOI), note-length histogram, note-length transition matrix. It also supports "relative metrics" that compare two sets (e.g. training vs generated). Note count, IOI and the note-length histogram/transition matrix are direct texture-vs-block-chord signals: block chords give long, uniform note lengths and a large IOI equal to the chord duration. | mgeval README https://github.com/RichardYang40148/mgeval ; paper "On the evaluation of generative models in music" DOI 10.1007/s00521-018-3849-7 | Neural Computing and Applications (Springer journal, peer-reviewed) | 2018 online / 2020 issue | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: high for the feature list, low for the OA/KLD formulas (the README does not show them) | class: metric-definition, code-availability
  - Overlapping Area (OA) = ∫ min(p_A(x), p_B(x)) dx between kernel-density estimates of a feature's intra-set and inter-set distance distributions, plus KL divergence. Unverified belief: not confirmed this run. The README points to mgeval/documentation.md, which I did not fetch. OA is defined between sets, not for a single pair, so it does not fit per-candidate scoring of 8 outputs unless you treat each candidate's bars as a set.
- **Originality report**: measures how much a generator copies compared with a baseline of how much human composers borrow from themselves and each other. Their Transformer fell below the baseline's 95% CI. Overfitting in later training epochs produced copies of input excerpts. Data and code are on OSF. | Yin et al., "Measuring When a Music Generation Algorithm Copies Too Much: The Originality Report, Cardinality Score, and Symbolic Fingerprinting by Geometric Hashing" https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/190005/ (Springer DOI 10.1007/s42979-022-01220-y) | SN Computer Science (peer-reviewed journal) | 2022 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: medium (from search summary; full text could not be parsed) | class: metric-definition, empirical-correlation, code-availability
  - Cardinality score, likely mechanism (unverified belief): the largest number of (onset, pitch) points shared under a translation in time and pitch, divided by the size of the excerpt. Because block chords are a point set, this would give a direct "fraction of prompt notes reproduced" with transposition and shift invariance. Pitfall (unverified): the score drops for random or sparse output, so on its own it rewards noise. Calibrate against a human-accompaniment baseline, as the paper does.
- **SSIMuse**: adapts the image Structural Similarity Index to symbolic piano-rolls to detect replication between generated and training pieces. Aimed at textured and expressive music that melody-focused measures handle poorly. It could compare a candidate piano-roll with the rendered block-chord piano-roll locally, catching shared notes and sustained durations at once. | Ji et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13658 | arXiv preprint | 2025-09 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: medium (from search summary) | class: metric-definition
- **Motif-level plagiarism**: plagiarism-like outputs from Transformer symbolic models are linked to local overfitting on frequently repeated motifs. Proposed fixes are label smoothing, transposition augmentation and Top-K sampling. Context for why a model replays its prompt. | Inaba & Inui, "Why Do Music Models Plagiarize? A Motif-Centric Perspective" https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/123788 | NeurIPS 2025 workshop (light peer review) | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: medium | class: empirical-correlation
- **Rhythm similarity vs perception**: the GrooveToolbox paper proposes new rhythm-similarity metrics for drum loops. A structural rhythmic similarity measure correlated more strongly with perceived similarity than the commonly used Hamming distance (which is essentially the XOR in GS). This suggests a GS score may not match what listeners hear as similar. | GrooveToolbox thesis / paper https://duo.uio.no/handle/10852/84395 | thesis/repository, venue unverified | 2021 | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: low-medium | class: empirical-correlation
- **mir_eval note precision/recall/F**: a note matches if the onset is within ±50 ms and the pitch within ±50 cents, with an optional offset criterion (within max(50 ms, 0.2 × reference duration)). An onset-only variant exists, plus an overlap-ratio score. This gives per-candidate copy rate = recall of prompt notes among candidate notes, and precision = share of candidate notes that are prompt copies. Unverified belief: the docs URL returned 404 this run. Recalled from training data; module `mir_eval.transcription`, functions `precision_recall_f1_overlap` and `onset_precision_recall_f1`, are unverified. | https://github.com/craffel/mir_eval (not fetched) | Raffel et al., ISMIR 2014 (peer-reviewed, unverified this run) | — | accessed 2026-11-22 | confidence: low | class: metric-definition
  - Pitfall: block chords are sustained, so a candidate that restrikes the same chord notes on every beat gets low offset-aware F but high onset-only recall at chord changes. Compute both onset-only and offset-aware scores and penalise the offset-aware match most heavily ("same notes, same onsets/durations"). Empty output gets precision 0/0, so define it as maximal penalty.

### Synthesis (my inference, not from a single source)
A copy penalty that allows partial reuse could combine:
- (a) offset-aware note F1 against the block-chord render, penalised only above a threshold such as 0.3;
- (b) GS (cosine variant) between candidate and prompt onset grids;
- (c) texture guards from the mgeval features: onset density relative to chord changes (onsets per chord span > 1), sustained-note ratio (share of notes lasting ≥ the chord span), and note-length histogram entropy.

Pair each with floors for note count and onset density so that sparse or random output is not rewarded.
