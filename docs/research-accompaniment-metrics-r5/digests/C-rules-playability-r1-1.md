# C: Harmony / voice-leading rule violations and piano playability (round 1, agent 1)

Accessed 2026-11-22. Web search/fetch only. 13 tool calls. Some claims come from search-result summaries rather than full-text reads; those are marked "snippet-level".

## Findings

### Voice-leading / part-writing checkers
- music21 `voiceLeading.VoiceLeadingQuartet` (two voices x two time points) has booleans for every core rule: `parallelFifth()` (parallel or antiparallel P5, octave displacement allowed), `parallelOctave()`, `parallelUnison()`, `parallelUnisonOrOctave()`, `hiddenFifth()`, `hiddenOctave()`, `hiddenInterval()`, `voiceCrossing()`, `voiceOverlap()`, `isProperResolution()` (dissonant P4/A4/d5/m7 resolve correctly), `leapNotSetWithStep()`, and motion types (parallel/similar/contrary/oblique). There is NO dedicated leading-tone or chordal-7th resolution method; `isProperResolution` covers the interval-level case only. Spacing checks were not seen in this module. `Verticality.isConsonant()` and `ThreeNoteLinearSegment.couldBePassingTone()/couldBeNeighborTone()` are also there. | music21 voiceLeading module docs, https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleVoiceLeading.html | official docs (MIT, open-source) | current docs | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | tool / code-availability
- Applying it to piano texture: the quartet API needs explicit voice pairs, so a non-4-voice piano accompaniment first needs voice separation (e.g. outer voices = bass and top note per onset, then pair bass with melody). ### Dissonance / consonance / tension
- Harrison & Pearce test 20 consonance models and argue that consonance is composite: interference (roughness), periodicity/harmonicity, and cultural familiarity. They propose a composite model and a corpus-familiarity model. The R package `incon` implements 17 models (e.g. Hutchinson-Knopoff roughness, harmonicity, corpus, composite). It takes symbolic pitch sets as input, so it can score each chord in a MIDI accompaniment. | "Simultaneous Consonance in Music Perception and Composition", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7032667 ; code https://github.com/pmcharrison/incon (README https://rdrr.io/github/pmcharrison/incon/f/README.md) | Psychological Review (peer-reviewed) | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | metric-definition / tool / code-availability
- Caveat: an independent poster (U. Minnesota) found surprisingly large disagreements between consonance models on dyads. Which model you pick matters, so report more than one. | https://conservancy.umn.edu/bitstream/handle/11299/223045/poster_final_final.pdf | poster (not peer-reviewed) | — | accessed 2026-11-22 | low (snippet-level) | empirical-correlation
- Herremans & Chew "Tension Ribbons" compute three tonal-tension measures on the Spiral Array over sliding windows. **Cloud diameter** is the maximal distance between notes in the window, i.e. dissonance. **Cloud momentum** is the movement of the window's center of effect, i.e. tonal change. **Tensile strain** is the distance of the center of effect from the key. MorpheuS uses them as generation targets. The one-line definitions come from my memory of the paper, and the retrieved page only names the three measures, so treat the definitions as partly unverified. | https://dorienherremans.com/content/tension-ribbons-quantifying-and-visualising-tonal-tension ; preprint https://www.dorienherremans.com/sites/default/files/paper_tenor_dh_preprint_small.pdf | TENOR 2016 (peer-reviewed conference) | 2016 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | metric-definition
- Sensory dissonance in Essentia is audio-only. For symbolic input you have to synthesize partials and apply Sethares/Plomp-Levelt yourself, or use `incon`. | search summary (Essentia docs not fetched) | — | — | accessed 2026-11-22 | low | tool

### Piano playability
- **PIG dataset + statistical fingering** (Nakamura, Saito, Yoshii): about 150 classical pieces with pianist fingerings, plus high-order HMM / DNN fingering estimators. A fingering model's likelihood or cost per note can serve as a difficulty or playability proxy. That use is my inference, not something the paper claims. | "Statistical Learning and Estimation of Piano Fingering", https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10237 ; demo https://statpianofingering.github.io/demo.html | Information Sciences 2020 (journal version: unverified belief); arXiv 2019 | 2019-04 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high (dataset/method); low (proxy use) | tool / code-availability
- ThumbSet (Ramoneda et al. 2022) is a larger, noisier fingering set. Pretraining on it and then fine-tuning on PIG beats the HMM baselines. | https://zenodo.org/records/6433702 | dataset (Zenodo) | 2022-04 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | tool
- RubricNet (Ramoneda et al.) is an interpretable symbolic piano-difficulty model on the CIPI dataset (652 pieces, 9 levels). Its descriptors are computed per hand. **Pitch entropy** is the Shannon entropy of pitches. **Pitch range** is max minus min MIDI pitch. **Average pitch** is mean MIDI pitch. **Displacement rate** is the weighted mean of the largest jump between consecutive pitch-set events (weight 0 under 7 semitones, 1 from 7 to 11, 2 from 12 up). **Average IOI** is in seconds, with 100 bpm assumed if no tempo. **Pitch-set LZ** is Lempel-Ziv complexity. It does NOT use hand span, polyphony or fingering cost. Code: https://github.com/pramoneda/rubricnet | "Towards Explainable and Interpretable Musical Difficulty Estimation", https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00473 | arXiv preprint (search summary says ISMIR 2024; not verified on page) | 2024-08 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | metric-definition / code-availability
- Ghatas, Fayek & Hadhoud split piano rolls into segments and train a hybrid DL classifier for difficulty. It is a black box, so it is less useful as an interpretable penalty. | https://doaj.org/article/d2a9c34b038643b5bf69cd32bc48ba2a | Alexandria Engineering Journal (peer-reviewed) | 2022-12 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | empirical-correlation
- Predicting difficulty from sheet images (Ramoneda et al.) and audio (the PSyllabus dataset, 7,901 pieces, 11 levels) are not directly applicable to MIDI. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16287 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03947 | arXiv / ISMIR-adjacent | 2023-2024 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | lead
- Accompaniment-generation papers (AccoMontage2, Florence Price art-song generator) evaluate with listening tests and perceived-error counts. The Price paper reports 45% error-free generations. Neither reports an automatic playability metric in the retrieved summaries. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00353 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23130 | ISMIR 2022 / arXiv 2025 | — | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium (snippet-level) | empirical-correlation

## Leads worth chasing
- Full text of Fang et al. 2020 ("Bach or Mock?") for its exact rule list, weights and any code (check the Pardo lab GitHub).
- Whether the Coconet / Bach Doodle paper (arXiv 1907.06637) or the DeepBach/BachBot evaluations count parallel-5th/8ve rates, and how often Bach himself breaks them (a baseline rate).
- Ramoneda et al. papers on fingering-based difficulty (e.g. "Score difficulty analysis for piano performance education based on fingering", ICASSP 2022) for a fingering-cost-as-difficulty formulation.
- Nakamura's PIG code/model release (the fingering HMM binary) to check whether per-note fingering cost can be pulled out.
- music21 `theoryAnalyzer` module (in alpha.theoryAnalysis) for batch counting of parallel fifths/octaves across a score; not checked this run.
- A voice-separation algorithm for piano (e.g. Chew & Wu contig mapping, or music21's `voiceLeading` on `chordify` outer voices) to make the quartet checks usable on accompaniment texture.
- `incon` model choice: compare `hutch_78_roughness`, `har_18_harmonicity` and `har_19_composite` on accompaniment chords.

## Looked for but not found
- A peer-reviewed automatic **piano playability metric** (hand span, per-hand polyphony, stretch) used to evaluate generated accompaniment. None turned up this run.
- A music21 method for leading-tone or chordal-7th resolution, or for upper-voice spacing (more than an octave between S-A or A-T). Not present in the voiceLeading module docs I read; `isProperResolution` is interval-level only.
- An evidence-backed recipe for applying 4-part voice-leading rules to free piano textures.
- A Python, symbolic-input sensory-dissonance library other than `incon` (which is R).
- Empirical correlation between rule-violation counts and human ratings of piano accompaniment specifically.
