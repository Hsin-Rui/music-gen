# Digest A — Harmonic fidelity metrics (r1-1)

Decision served: objective metrics for choosing the best of 8 generated symbolic piano accompaniments, given a melody and a FIXED per-beat chord chart. This digest covers only how well each accompaniment realises the chord chart.
Run budget: 13 tool calls, 9 sources touched. Accessed: 2026-11-22. All claims come from retrieved pages unless they are tagged [reasoning] or [unverified belief].

## Findings

- **CTnCTR (chord tone to non-chord tone ratio)** = (n_c + n_p) / (n_c + n_n). Here n_c is the number of chord tones, n_n the number of non-chord tones, and n_p the "proper" non-chord tones that lie within 2 semitones of the following note. It equals 1 when there are no non-chord tones. Higher is better. It was defined for the MELODY against the chords, not for an accompaniment. | Automatic Melody Harmonization with Triad Chords: A Comparative Study (Yeh et al.) https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02360 (formula as relayed by search over 2001.02360 / 2202.08423) | Journal of New Music Research 50(1):37–51 (peer-reviewed) | 2021 (arXiv 2020) | accessed 2026-11-22 | med (the PDF could not be parsed, so the formula comes from a search summary, not the paper text) | metric-definition
- **PCS (pitch consonance score)**: the consonance of intervals between melody notes and chord notes, averaged over 16th-note windows. Unisons, thirds, fifths and sixths count as consonant; seconds, sevenths and tritones as dissonant. **MCTD**: the duration-weighted mean distance in the 6-D tonal space (Harte et al. 2006) between each melody note's pitch-class vector and its chord label. Lower is better. | Same Yeh et al. paper; descriptions repeated in A Survey on Evaluation Metrics for Music Generation https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051 and in https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07627 | JNMR (peer-reviewed); the survey and 2512.07627 are arXiv preprints | 2021 / 2025 / 2025 | accessed 2026-11-22 | med | metric-definition
- **Six objective metrics were paired with a 202-participant subjective study.** I found no metric-to-rating correlation on the abstract page. A later paper's search snippet reports a "harmonicity" score correlating with CTnCTR (r = 0.90), with PCS (r = 0.88) and with MCTD (r = −0.86). That is a correlation among metrics, not with listeners, and the paper is probably 2202.08423. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02360 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08423 | JNMR; arXiv | 2021; 2022 | accessed 2026-11-22 | low (the r values were not seen in the original text, and it is unclear which paper reports them) | empirical-correlation
- **No code repository is linked on the Yeh et al. arXiv page.** The later harmonization paper 2512.07627 links https://github.com/NeuraLLMuse/BStarConstraintHarmonization, but it is not stated whether that repo implements the metrics. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02360 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07627 | — | — | accessed 2026-11-22 | med | code-availability
- **Chord Accuracy (CA), original form**: CA = 1/(N_tracks·N_chords) · Σ_i Σ_j 1{C_ij = Ĉ_ij}. Chords are recognised per track and per bar from the generated output and compared with the conditioning chords. | PopMAG: Pop Music Accompaniment Generation https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07703 (formula via the 2509.00051 survey) | ACM Multimedia 2020 (peer-reviewed) [venue is unverified belief; not confirmed this run] | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | med | metric-definition
- **Chord Accuracy, whole-arrangement form**: CA = (1/N_chord) Σ 1{C_i = Ĉ_i}. Chords are extracted from the full generated arrangement with Jiang et al.'s large-vocabulary chord transcription. The match is on root and quality at 1-beat granularity, which the authors call stricter than PopMAG's. The same paper's "Degree of Arrangement" uses KL divergence between pitch-class histograms, but that measures diversity across tracks, not chord fidelity. Subjective results (23 participants, RM-ANOVA) "align" with the objective ones only qualitatively; no correlation is reported. | Structured Multi-Track Accompaniment Arrangement via Style Prior Modelling (Zhao et al.) https://arxiv.org/html/2310.16334v4 | NeurIPS 2024 (peer-reviewed) | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | metric-definition
- **No code repo for the system is linked**, only a demo page at https://zhaojw1998.github.io/structured-arrangement/. | same | NeurIPS 2024 | 2024 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | code-availability
- **Piano accompaniment from a lead sheet uses four metrics.** (a) Chord Accuracy: exact beat-level match between chords extracted with Dai et al.'s rule-based recogniser and the reference chords. (b) Chord Similarity: cosine similarity of two-bar chord embeddings from a pre-trained chord encoder (Wang et al. 2020, EC²-VAE/poly-dis family), which gives partial credit for shared chord tones. (c) Out-of-key ratio. (d) Grooving similarity. Root, quality and bass handling are not specified. Results on POP909-test, CA/CS in %: ground truth 91.6/95.7, Polyffusion 37.5/54.0, D3PIA 80.1/93.6, WSG-4th (whole-song hierarchical generation) 87.6/94.6, FGG 62.0/77.3. | D3PIA: A Discrete Denoising Diffusion Model for Piano Accompaniment Generation From Lead Sheet (Choi et al., KAIST) https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03523 | arXiv preprint (no venue stated) | 2026-02 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | metric-definition
- **Evidence that CA/CS and perceived harmony come apart.** WSG-4th had higher CA and CS than D3PIA but a lower subjective Harmony rating. Several of its outputs had CS above 0.95 yet were rated about 3 out of 5. The authors attribute this to dissonance and point to its higher out-of-key ratio (2.4% vs 0%). The listening test had 13 participants and 10 excerpts; no correlation statistic is given. Implication: chord-match alone is not sufficient, and an out-of-key or dissonance penalty is needed alongside it. | D3PIA https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03523 | arXiv | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-22 | med (small N, qualitative) | empirical-correlation
- **Code**: training code and samples are stated to be on https://jech2.github.io/D3PIA/. The Polyffusion code is at https://github.com/aik2mlj/polyffusion (ISMIR 2023). | D3PIA; Polyffusion https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.10304 | arXiv; ISMIR 2023 (peer-reviewed) | 2026; 2023 | accessed 2026-11-22 | med | code-availability
- **Chroma similarity per bar**: s_chr = 100·⟨r^a, r^b⟩ / (‖r^a‖‖r^b‖), where r is a 12-dimensional chroma vector of onset counts. To use it for chord fidelity, take r^b as the chord-template chroma [reasoning]. | A Survey on Evaluation Metrics for Music Generation https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051 (attributes it to Wang et al. 2024) | arXiv survey | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-22 | med | metric-definition

It warns that feature metrics are "no way sufficient" for overall quality. | https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00051 | arXiv | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | empirical-correlation
- **Other harmony suites appear in accompaniment papers.** SongDriver uses chord pitch interval, CTnCTR, melody-chord consonance and chord stability. ReaLchords uses the note-in-chord ratio. | https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.06054 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14723 (search summaries only) | arXiv (SongDriver: ACM MM 2022 [unverified belief]) | 2022; 2025 | accessed 2026-11-22 | low | metric-definition
- **Sparse output** [reasoning, not evidenced in sources]:
  - Chord-tone ratios (CTR, CTnCTR) and PCS are normalised by note count. An accompaniment of a few sustained chord tones therefore scores close to perfect, so these metrics reward sparse or blocky output.
  - Recogniser-based CA/CS should penalise very sparse output on beats with too few notes to recognise, but they reward root-position block chords.
  - Pair them with a density or texture term, or with a coverage term: the fraction of chord-chart beats where the chord's pitch classes are actually present.

## Bass / root on downbeats (Q3)
- **Root is matched, but there is no separate bass or inversion criterion.** The only retrieved metric that states its matching criterion (Zhao et al., NeurIPS 2024) compares root + quality. Root here means the chord-label root, not the sounding bass note. D3PIA does not specify root/quality/bass handling. | high | metric-definition
- **I found no published bass-correctness or root-on-downbeat metric** for accompaniment generation in this run (see "Looked for but not found").
- [reasoning] A large-vocabulary recogniser such as Jiang et al. outputs inversions (e.g. C/E), so whether bass matters depends on whether slash chords are collapsed before comparison. Wrong-bass realisations of a chart are perceptually salient. For a fixed chart, a simple custom metric (the lowest sounding pitch class on each chart-chord onset equals the chart's bass or root) is cheap to add, but it is unvalidated.

## Leads worth chasing
- Read Yeh et al. JNMR 2021 itself (DOI under 10.1080/09298215.2021.x) to confirm the exact CTnCTR, PCS and MCTD formulas and whether any metric–listener correlation is reported.
- The Whole-Song Hierarchical Generation paper (Wang, Min, Xia, ICLR 2024; "WSG"): check its chord evaluation and code (likely in the ZZWaang GitHub repos) [unverified belief].
- The Dai et al. rule-based chord extractor used by D3PIA, and the Jiang et al. large-vocabulary chord transcription: confirm code availability. These would serve as the recogniser that turns a generated accompaniment into a chord sequence to compare with the chart.
- Wang et al. 2020 chord encoder (poly-dis / EC²-VAE): reuse it for the Chord Similarity metric.
- FIGARO (von Rütte et al., ICLR 2023) "chord accuracy" description metric; GETMusic; AccoMontage / AccoMontage2 chord evaluation. None were fetched this run.
- "Local chord corruption is not recognizer replay" (arXiv 2609.03584, preprint, code at https://github.com/Viwennnnnn/local-chord-corruption-replay) argues that response magnitude and output-chord agreement should be reported together. It is tangential: audio, MIDI-SAG and MusicGen-Chord.
- Accompaniment Prompt Adherence (arXiv 2503.06346) is validated against human judgments, but it is audio and CLAP-based.
