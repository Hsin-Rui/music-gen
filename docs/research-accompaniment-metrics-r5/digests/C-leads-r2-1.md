# C-leads round 2, part 1: rule-violation and playability checks (best-of-8 piano accompaniment)

Brief: objective rule-violation and playability checks on symbolic MIDI (melody and chord chart given). Web only. 14 tool calls. Several PDFs (arXiv 2107.10388, CMMR 2023 zenodo) could not be parsed because no local PDF tool was available, so I could not read them.

## Findings

- **Correction to the brief.** The authors of "Bach or Mock?" are Alexander Fang, Alisa Liu, Prem Seetharaman and Bryan Pardo. No "Lin" is listed. The venue is the ML4MD Workshop at ICML 2020, a 2-page paper. | arXiv abs page https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13329 | ICML 2020 ML4MD workshop | 2020-06-23 (v3 2020-07-17) | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | bibliographic
- **Bach or Mock features.** There are six feature distributions: (1) pitch, as scale degrees with spelling kept and octave ignored; (2) rhythm, as durations in quarter-note units; (3) directed melodic intervals, with one distribution for each of S, A, T and B; (4) harmonic quality, as chord-quality distribution; (5) **parallel errors**, as counts of parallel 5ths, 8ves and unisons in similar and contrary motion; (6) lengths of repeated note sequences (Hsu et al. 1998). **Voice crossing is not a feature.** | arXiv HTML v3 https://arxiv.org/html/2006.13329v3 | ICML 2020 ML4MD | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | method
- **How Bach or Mock combines them.** It is built on music21. For each feature it takes the Wasserstein distance from the reference distribution of the Bach corpus. The grade is a weighted sum of those distances (weights are mostly 1), and lower is better. The parallel-error term is scaled by (the piece's error-to-note ratio) / (Bach's ratio). That per-note normalisation can be reused directly for counting violations across 8 candidates. | same | | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | method
- **Bach or Mock validation.** Median grade was 4.91 for Bach and 8.94 for generated chorales. On 3 paired discriminations, the grader picked the Bach chorale 92% of the time, against 86.7% for "experts". There were 36 listeners, but only 5 experts. This is a discrimination task, not a correlation with quality ratings. | same | | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium (small n) | evidence
- **Bach or Mock code.** Code exists at https://github.com/asdfang/constraint-transformer-bach/tree/master/Grader. I did not inspect it. | arXiv HTML v3 | | 2020 | accessed 2026-11-22 | high (that it exists) | code
- **Coconet / Bach Doodle.** The authors examined parallel 5ths and 8ves in Coconet output. They found such parallels were common in the training data, and with no key or time signatures in that data the model probably learned them as acceptable. Note the caveat: a raw parallel count can flag patterns that exist in the reference corpus itself. I saw this only in a search snippet and did not read the paper. | "The Bach Doodle…" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.06637 | arXiv / ISMIR 2019 | 2019 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | evidence
- **CMMR 2023 paper.** It adds a training loss that penalises prohibited patterns such as parallel 5ths and 8ves. I could not read the title, authors or numbers. | https://zenodo.org/records/10112462/files/cmmr2023_2c-P5.pdf | CMMR 2023 | 2023 | accessed 2026-11-22 | low | lead
- **music21 `voiceLeading.VoiceLeadingQuartet(v1n1, v1n2, v2n1, v2n2, key)`.** Voice 1 is the upper voice. It provides `parallelFifth()` (which includes antiparallel fifths and fifths displaced by an octave), `parallelOctave()`, `parallelUnison()`, `parallelUnisonOrOctave()`, `parallelInterval()`, `hiddenFifth()` and `hiddenOctave()` (the docs say these find *all* hidden intervals, not only the forbidden ones), `voiceCrossing()` and `voiceOverlap()`. Documented helper: `iterateAllVoiceLeadingQuartets()`. It works on pairs of **monophonic** voice lines, so piano chords must first be split into lines. | music21 voiceLeading docs https://music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleVoiceLeading.html | official docs | current | accessed 2026-11-22 | high | tool
- **music21 theoryAnalyzer.** The docs say only that theoryAnalyzer "divides a score up into these segments" of VLQ objects. I could not confirm the `identifyParallelFifths` signature: the docs page does not list it and the raw GitHub path returned 404. **Unverified belief:** it lives in `music21.alpha.theoryAnalysis.theoryAnalyzer`, takes partNum pairs and stores results via `addAnalysisData`. `music21.figuredBass.checker` also exists as source and is a likely alternative. | music21 docs (above); search result https://music21.org/music21docs/_modules/music21/figuredBass/checker.html | | | accessed 2026-11-22 | low | tool
- **McLeod & Steedman HMM voice separation.** Published in JNMR 2016. It separates MIDI, including live performance MIDI, into monophonic voices and was evaluated on Bach pieces against voices taken from the score. Code and data are at https://github.com/apmcleod/voice-splitting. Their 2018 paper notes there is no standard voice-separation metric and uses average voice consistency and precision/recall/F1. Chew & Wu (2005) and Jordanous (2008) are the baselines compared. | "HMM-Based Voice Separation of MIDI Performance" https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/hmm-based-voice-separation-of-midi-performance/ ; ISMIR 2018 https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/148_Paper.pdf | JNMR 2016; ISMIR 2018 | 2016/2018 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium (snippets) | tool/evidence
- **Ramoneda piano difficulty.** The ICASSP 2022 paper is "Score difficulty analysis for piano performance education based on fingering". It estimates difficulty from *predicted fingering*, not from explicit hand-span features. The follow-up "Combining piano performance dimensions for score difficulty classification" (ESWA 2024, doi 10.1016/j.eswa.2023.121776; arXiv 2306.08480) uses pretrained fingering and expressiveness models on the CIPI dataset: 652 pieces, 9 levels, 39.5% balanced accuracy. These are learned embeddings and give no explicit hand-span, chord-size or speed thresholds. | UPF repository https://repositori.upf.edu/handle/10230/53378 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.08480 | ICASSP 2022; ESWA 2024 | 2022/2024 | accessed 2026-11-22 | medium | evidence

## Leads
- Read `Grader/` in asdfang/constraint-transformer-bach. It is the reference implementation for counting parallel errors with music21 and normalising per note.
- arXiv 2511.16228: find its playability definition. It may contain explicit hand-span or polyphony rules.
- arXiv 2408.00473 ("Towards Explainable and Interpretable Musical [difficulty]…", Ramoneda group?) may contain explicit interpretable difficulty features.
- arXiv 2107.10388 (JS Fake Chorales): listeners were near chance. Check whether it reports any rule counts.
- ISMIR 2017 paper 000026 and de Valk's thesis (City, Univ. London) on voice separation came up in the search and were not read.
- music21 `figuredBass.checker` source: possibly a ready-made checker for parallels across parts.

## Looked for but not found
- Explicit hand-span, chord-size or speed features in the Ramoneda papers. None appeared in what I retrieved; their work uses learned fingering embeddings.
- A validated skyline-algorithm baseline for extracting outer voices from piano MIDI. No source retrieved. **Unverified belief:** for piano accompaniment, taking the highest and lowest sounding note per onset (skyline and "floor") is the simplest way to get the outer voices. No validation evidence was retrieved this run.
- Studies linking rule-violation counts to listening ratings for TonicNet, DeepBach or BachBot (2021–2025). None were retrieved. Only Bach or Mock's discrimination accuracy is evidenced.
- VoSA; Chew & Wu contig mapping details (it appeared only as a baseline name).
- The signature and documentation of music21 `theoryAnalyzer.identifyParallelFifths` (404 / not indexed).
